***NOTE :***- I'm sharing the strategy that worked for me, based on my actual experience. I'm not copying someone else's strategy or claiming this is the only way to prepare. These are the things I personally believe every serious aspirant should focus on.

Let's divide this into two parts: ***Prelims Killer*** and ***Mains Killer.***

### PART 1: PRELIMS KILLER

- 
***RRB PO Prelims consists of:***

- ***Reasoning***: 40 questions

- ***Quantitative Aptitude***: 40 questions

- ***Total***: 80 questions

- ***Time***: 45 minutes overall

There is no English section in Prelims. This means you cannot rely on English to compensate for a weak Reasoning or Quant score.

**Your focus has to be on maximising your performance in these two sections ( Tagdi speed or accuracy target krni hai )**

2. ***REASONING — Target 40/40***

A) This is the section where I was most confident. I scored 40/40 in both my previous attempts in this section.

B) My advice is to prepare with the mindset that you should be able to solve all 40 questions. Don't leave an entire topic just because it feels difficult.

***What should you do?***

**Puzzles and Seating Arrangement**

- Identify your weakest puzzle types and practise at least four puzzles of those types daily.

- Don't solve puzzles just to complete a daily target. Analyse the logic, the cases you missed and the mistakes you made.

- Practise enough that you can solve familiar puzzles confidently without repeatedly starting over.

- If a puzzle seems unusually tricky, don't waste excessive time on it. Mark it for review and move ahead.

**Meaningful Word**

Attempt these towards the end. There are methods using letter combinations that help you identify possible meaningful words systematically.

You can check the explanation by ***Maths Mania*** on YouTube and practise that approach to improve your accuracy.

Which teacher should you follow?

You can follow Sanjay Sir or Puneet Sir for Reasoning. I've personally been following Sanjay Sir for some time. His content is excellent, particularly for understanding attempting strategies — which questions to pick first and which ones to leave for later.

He can be a little strict sometimes, but the content is worth it.

Sectional mocks: Start alternating between Reasoning and Quant sectional mocks. Analyse every mock instead of simply checking your score.

3. QUANT — Master Speed and Question Selection

For Quant, I personally recommend Harshul Agrawal. His content has worked very well for me.

My recommended order

- 
Simplification/Approximation

- 
Wrong Number Series

- 
Quadratic Equations

- 
An easy DI set that you can confidently solve

- 
The remaining questions according to your strengths and the time available

Don't get stuck on a DI just because you've already started it. If the calculations are taking too long, move on.

Arithmetic is important

You need a good grip over Arithmetic, not just the short-question topics.

Focus on topics such as:

- Profit & Loss

- Average

- Problems on Ages

- Boats & Streams

- Mensuration

- Other standard Arithmetic topics

Make your strongest topics exceptionally strong, but don't completely ignore your weaker ones. Standard questions from these topics are worth preparing because the same concepts also help in Mains.

The difficulty can vary from shift to shift. Your job is to identify the questions you can solve accurately and quickly.

Remember: You don't need to solve every difficult question. You need to avoid losing easy marks and wasting time on questions that aren't worth the effort.

PART 2: MAINS KILLER 🔥

Prelims and Mains require different approaches. In Mains, you need a subject-wise plan and consistent revision.

My biggest advice is this: ***if your Maths and Reasoning are average, maximise your score in*** **English and Computer** ***while keeping the other sections competitive. Don't neglect GA, because it can make a major difference to your overall score.***

- 
***REASONING*** — Concept Clarity + Attempting Strategy

Follow either Sanjay Sir or Puneet Sir, depending on whose teaching style suits you.

I personally follow Sanjay Sir.

- Work on your weak puzzle types instead of repeatedly solving only the ones you already know.

- Learn question selection and time management from your teacher.

- Improve Critical Reasoning. Some questions are direct, and you shouldn't miss easy marks because your concepts are unclear.

- Practise sectional tests and analyse the questions where your approach failed.

The objective is not just to solve difficult questions. It is to maximise your score within the available time.

2. ***QUANT*** — Arithmetic + DI + Calculation Efficiency

For Quant, my recommendation is Harshul Agrawal.

Build your Arithmetic concepts properly because they help in both Prelims and Mains.

- Strengthen your core Arithmetic topics.

- Practise DI regularly.

- Improve calculation speed and accuracy.

- Learn to identify which questions deserve your time.

- Analyse your mistakes after every sectional mock.

Don't spend all your preparation time on difficult questions while leaving standard question types underprepared.

3. ***ENGLISH*** — Follow Rani Mam

For English, follow Rani Mam and stay consistent with your preparation. ( Nimisha mam is also OG )

Don't rely only on your existing English level. Mains requires practice, accuracy and the ability to handle lengthy or confusing questions under pressure.

- Follow the classes consistently.

- Practise the question types covered in class.

- Work on your weak areas rather than repeatedly attempting only your strongest topics.

- Take sectional mocks and analyse your errors.

- Revise the concepts and approaches you've learned.

English can be a major scoring opportunity if you prepare systematically. Take full advantage of it.

4. COMPUTER ( GAME CHANGER ) — Follow Sachin Arora Sir

### Computer is one of the subjects where you should try to maximise your score.

- Follow Sachin Arora Sir's classes.

- Complete the topics systematically.

- Revise the material regularly instead of watching lectures and forgetting the facts.

- Practise questions and revisit whatever you get wrong.

- Keep a record of completed topics and pending revision.

Don't treat Computer as a subject you can finish casually at the last minute. Prepare it seriously so that you can make the most of the marks available.

5. ***GA*** — Follow SHeetal Ma'am and Revise at Least 3 Times

I'm personally following SHeetal Ma'am for GA. Her classes were useful for my IBPS PO Mains preparation, too.

My approach is straightforward:

- Target two lectures daily at 2x speed, depending on the lecture length and your available time.

- Maintain a proper record of completed lectures.

- Revise every lecture at least three times before the exam.

- Don't confuse completing lectures with completing preparation. Revision is essential for retaining facts.

***Current Affairs Window***

If your exam is in December, prepare the last four months of current affairs thoroughly. Include November and continue coverage through approximately 10 December, adjusting this to the actual exam date and the relevant exam's current-affairs window.

Don't postpone all your revision until the last week.

### FINAL WORDS

RRB PO is an achievable target, but you need consistency, strong fundamentals, mock analysis and revision.

You don't need to keep changing teachers and strategies every few days. Choose resources that work for you, follow them properly and focus on improving your actual performance.
